RGB is a cube

R, G, and B are Cartesian axes. The point is simple; the volume that means “red under many lighting conditions” is not.

Explore one RGB slice

Drag across red and green while blue stays fixed at 53. A slice makes a 3-D cube editable on a 2-D screen.

HSV as a teaching cone

Hue is an angle. Saturation moves away from gray. Value moves upward. Here radius is S × V, so the available chroma visibly collapses near black.

Hue–saturation disk

Drag on the disk to choose hue and saturation. Its radius shrinks with V: at low light many nominal hues produce nearly the same dark RGB values.

Two important failure regions

Low saturation

Near the gray axis, tiny sensor or lighting changes can swing the reported hue. Use S to decide whether H carries meaning.

Low value

Near black, the channels have few useful levels. The cone squeezes because the maximum possible RGB channel difference is small.

RGB565 quantization

The firmware receives 5 red bits, 6 green bits, and 5 blue bits—not the exact color selected above. The readouts show both.

From a picture to a predicate

Ask simpler questions of better coordinates

A deliberately crude RGB question

R > 96
R > 1.35 × G
R > 1.35 × B

This is difficult to tune across shade, glare, and tinted illumination. It is an illustration, not a recommended detector.

An HSV composition

angle(H, red) < 30°
S > 30%
V > 25%

Hue describes the family; S and V reject places where hue is weak evidence. Red is merely today's example.

The embedded transform

features = rgb565_to_yhsv[pixel]

Every possible 16-bit pixel already has one packed Y/H/S/V answer. Memory replaces repeated per-pixel divisions and floating-point work.
